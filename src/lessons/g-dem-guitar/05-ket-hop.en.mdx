You know the rhythms; now make your accompaniment **seamless and expressive**.

## Changing chords without losing the beat

The most common mistake: stopping the strumming hand so the fretting hand has time to change chords. Do the opposite — **the strumming hand never stops**:

- Use **the last step of the bar** (usually the final upstroke) to lift your fretting hand to the new chord. That strum may ring open strings — that's fine, everyone does it.
- Practise **"dry" chord changes**: fretting hand only, switching between two chords with a metronome, once per beat.
- Look for **common fingers**: C → Am keeps fingers 1 and 2 in place and moves only finger 3.

## Colour notes: sus and add9

Adding or replacing a note while you play makes the guitar "sparkle":

- **D → Dsus4 → D**: lift and replace your little finger on string 1 fret 3 — a "pull" that resolves back home. Great at the end of a phrase.
- **C → Cadd9**: add finger 4 on string 2 fret 3.

## Arranging by song section

A good song has a structure of **intro – verse – chorus – (bridge) – outro**. Changing your accompaniment by section gives the song a build:

| Section | Suggested accompaniment |
|---|---|
| Intro | Slow arpeggios, or the chorus chords played gently |
| Verse | **Picking** — light, so the vocals stand out |
| Chorus | **Strumming** — fuller and stronger |
| Bridge | Change the rhythm, or strum one long chord per bar |
| Outro | Arpeggios, slowing down, letting the last chord ring |
