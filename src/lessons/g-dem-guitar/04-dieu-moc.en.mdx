Fingerpicking gives a soft, clear accompaniment — perfect for verses, slow songs, or singing on your own.

<Callout type="note" title="Reminder: one finger per string">
The thumb (p) takes the bass B / B′ on strings 6 – 5 – 4; the index (i) string 3; the middle (m) string 2; the ring finger (a) string 1. Keep your wrist relaxed and **pull** the strings towards your palm rather than flicking outwards.
</Callout>

## Ballad picking (4/4)

**B – 3 – 2 – 3 – 1 – 3 – 2 – 3**. The most common picking pattern — get this one solid first.

<RhythmPlayer rhythm="ballad-pick" progression="C G Am Em F C F G" />

## Slow picking (4/4)

Even upward arpeggios with the alternate bass on beat 3: **B – 3 – 2 – 1 – B′ – 3 – 2 – 1**.

<RhythmPlayer rhythm="slow-pick" progression="Am F C G" />

## Waltz picking (3/4)

**B – 3 – 2 – 1 – 2 – 3**: one bass note, then up and back down — exactly 6 notes for 3 beats.

<RhythmPlayer rhythm="valse-pick" progression="C Am Dm G" />

## Slow rock picking (12/8)

Triplets: **B 3 2 – 1 2 3 – B′ 3 2 – 1 2 3**.

<RhythmPlayer rhythm="slowrock-pick" progression="C Em Am G" />

## Travis picking

The folk and country technique: **the thumb plays a steady alternating bass on every beat** (B – B′ – B – B′) like a drum, while the other fingers pick in between. A bit harder — practise the thumb part alone until it is perfectly steady.

<RhythmPlayer rhythm="travis" progression="C Am F G" tempo={80} />

## Arpeggios

Up and back down: **B – 4 – 3 – 2 – 1 – 2 – 3 – 4**. Great for intros and outros.

<RhythmPlayer rhythm="arpeggio" progression="Em C G D" />
