Congratulations — you have completed the curriculum! The final project is **a short 16-bar song** (8-bar verse + 8-bar chorus) that you write, accompany and solo over yourself.

## Your checklist

1. **Choose a key and a mood**: happy or sad? Which key suits your voice? (Use **Tools → Keys** to see the diatonic chords.)
2. **Write the progression** for the verse and chorus (the "Song structure" lesson). Add one unusual colour: a 7th chord, a sus chord, a borrowed chord or a secondary dominant.
3. **Write the melody**: a hook for the chorus, chord tones on strong beats, lyrics (if any) that fit the words.
4. **Choose an accompaniment**: picking for the verse, strumming for the chorus (guitar track) — or two-hand voicings (piano).
5. **Solo**: write or improvise an 8-bar solo over the chorus progression, using a motif and chord tones.
6. **Record** the whole song on your phone. Listen again the next day and fix one thing you're not happy with.

## Example: a song in G

Verse (8 bars): **Em – C – G – D** × 2. Chorus (8 bars): **C – D – G – Em – C – D – G – G**.

Over the chorus progression in G: use **G major pentatonic** and end each phrase on a chord tone (B over G, E over Em, C over C, F♯ or A over D). Try a chromatic approach note before each chord tone on beat 1, and a motif that you vary across the 4 chords.
</Callout>

## What next?

- Review daily on the **Review** page to keep your knowledge fresh.
- Analyse your favourite songs: find the key, the Roman numerals, borrowed chords and secondary dominants.
- Play with other people: jamming is the fastest way to learn.
- Come back to any module at any time — you will understand it more deeply each time.
