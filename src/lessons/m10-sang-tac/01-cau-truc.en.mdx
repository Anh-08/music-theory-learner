By now you have all the "ingredients": scales, chords, progressions, harmonic colours. The final module puts them all together into **a song**.

## The sections of a song

| Section | Role |
|---|---|
| Intro | Sets the mood |
| Verse | Tells the story; lower melody, moderate energy |
| Pre-chorus | A transition that gradually builds tension |
| Chorus | The main idea, the "hook" — the climax and the most memorable part |
| Bridge | A one-time change of mood before the final chorus |
| Outro | Ends the song |

Two common forms: **Verse – Chorus – Verse – Chorus – Bridge – Chorus** (pop) and **AABA** (many classic songs and jazz standards).

## Writing a progression for each section

Tricks for creating **contrast** between verse and chorus:

- **Start on different chords**: begin the verse on **vi** (sad, storytelling) and the chorus on **IV** or **I** (opening up, bright).
- **Harmonic rhythm**: two bars per chord in the verse, one bar per chord in the chorus → the chorus feels more driving.
- **Half cadence – full cadence**: end the verse on **V** (unresolved, waiting for the chorus) and the chorus on **I**.
- **Use unusual colours in the bridge**: borrowed chords (♭VI, ♭VII, iv) or secondary dominants (Module 9).
