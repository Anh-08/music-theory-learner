You have played dozens of chords. This lesson shows you **what is inside them** — so you can build any chord yourself instead of looking it up.

## Stacking thirds

A **triad** has three notes: the **root** (1), a **3rd** and a **5th** above the root. In other words: take a note, stack a 3rd on it, then stack another 3rd.

There are two kinds of 3rd (major and minor), so there are exactly **four** ways to stack them:

| Type | Formula | Stack | Root C | Symbol |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Major | 1 – 3 – 5 | major 3rd + minor 3rd | C E G | C |
| Minor | 1 – ♭3 – 5 | minor 3rd + major 3rd | C E♭ G | Cm |
| Diminished | 1 – ♭3 – ♭5 | minor 3rd + minor 3rd | C E♭ G♭ | Cdim, C° |
| Augmented | 1 – 3 – ♯5 | major 3rd + major 3rd | C E G♯ | Caug, C+ |

<Show root="C" chord="m" labels="interval" caption="C minor: 1 – ♭3 – 5 — only one note different from C major" />

Hear all four: <Play notes="C4 E4 G4" as="both">Major</Play> <Play notes="C4 Eb4 G4" as="both">Minor</Play> <Play notes="C4 Eb4 Gb4" as="both">Diminished</Play> <Play notes="C4 E4 G#4" as="both">Augmented</Play>

<Callout type="note" title="The 3rd decides the colour">
Major and minor differ only in the **3rd**: lower it by a half step and the chord turns from bright to sad. The perfect 5th is the solid "floor". A diminished chord (♭5) sounds tense and unstable; an augmented chord (♯5) sounds suspended, as if waiting for something.
</Callout>

## Looking inside familiar chords

<OnlyFor instrument="guitar">

You have been playing E and Em since your first days. Look at which degrees their notes are:

<Show root="E" chord="M" shape="022100" labels="interval" caption="E (022100): E – B – E – G♯ – B – E. Six strings but only three notes: 1, 3, 5" />

<Show root="E" chord="m" shape="022000" labels="interval" caption="Em (022000): lift the finger on string 3 → G♯ becomes G, the major 3rd becomes a minor 3rd" />

A six-string chord is just **three notes doubled** in several octaves. When you build your own chords, just make sure 1, 3 and 5 are there.

</OnlyFor>

<OnlyFor instrument="piano">

On piano, a major chord is **4 + 3** half steps and a minor chord is **3 + 4**. Try it on D:
