A C chord is C – E – G, but those three notes don't have to be stacked in that order. **Which note is lowest** (the bass note) decides the chord's **inversion**:

| Inversion | Lowest note | Order (low → high) | Symbol |
|---|---|---|---|
| Root position | Root (1) | C – E – G | C |
| 1st inversion | The 3rd | E – G – C | C/E |
| 2nd inversion | The 5th | G – C – E | C/G |

**C/E** reads "C over E": a C chord with E as the lowest note. You will see this kind of symbol all the time on chord sheets.

On guitar, triads on the **top three strings** (strings 3, 2, 1) are a great way to see inversions — and a secret weapon when playing in a band:

<Show root="C" chord="M" shape="x,x,x,9,8,8" frets={15} labels="interval" caption="1st inversion (frets 9 – 8 – 8): E – G – C" />

<Show root="C" chord="M" shape="x,x,x,12,13,12" frets={15} labels="interval" caption="2nd inversion (frets 12 – 13 – 12): G – C – E" />

The three shapes connect up the neck: after the 2nd inversion you reach root position again an octave higher.

</OnlyFor>

## Why inversions? Smooth chord changes

Play C → F → G → C in root position: your hand jumps around quite a bit. With inversions, each note moves **as little as possible** — this is called **voice leading**:

<OnlyFor instrument="piano">

<Show notes="C4 E4 G4" labels="note" play="chord" caption="C (root position)" />

<Show notes="C4 F4 A4" labels="note" play="chord" caption="F/C (2nd inversion): C stays, E goes up to F, G up to A" />

<Show notes="B3 D4 G4" labels="note" play="chord" caption="G/B (1st inversion): C goes down to B, F down to D, A down to G" />

Your hand hardly moves yet the chords change — it sounds as smooth as a choir.

</OnlyFor>

<OnlyFor instrument="guitar">

<Show root="C" chord="M" shape="x,x,x,5,5,3" frets={15} labels="note" caption="C (root position): frets 5 – 5 – 3" />

<Show root="F" chord="M" shape="x,x,x,5,6,5" frets={15} labels="note" caption="F/C (2nd inversion): frets 5 – 6 – 5" />

<Show root="G" chord="M" shape="x,x,x,4,3,3" frets={15} labels="note" caption="G/B (1st inversion): frets 4 – 3 – 3" />

All three shapes sit between frets 3 and 6. Loop C → F → G → C: it sounds far more "pro" than three open chords.

</OnlyFor>

## A descending bass line

Inversions also let the bass note form a melodic line. A very common ballad progression: **C → G/B → Am** — the bass steps down C → B → A: <Play notes="C3 B2 A2">bass C – B – A</Play>
