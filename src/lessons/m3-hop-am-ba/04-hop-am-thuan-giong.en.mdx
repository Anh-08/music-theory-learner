This is the most important lesson of Module 3. Once you understand it, you will see why each song uses only a certain group of chords — and be able to guess the next chord yourself.

## Building a chord on each degree

Take the C major scale. On each degree, build a triad by stacking 3rds — but **using only notes from the scale** (take every other note):

| Degree | Notes | Chord | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | C – E – G | C | Major |
| ii | D – F – A | Dm | Minor |
| iii | E – G – B | Em | Minor |
| IV | F – A – C | F | Major |
| V | G – B – D | G | Major |
| vi | A – C – E | Am | Minor |
| vii° | B – D – F | B° | Diminished |

Listen in order: <PlayChords chords="C Dm Em F G Am Bdim C" seconds={1.1} />

These are the **7 diatonic chords** of C major. Most songs in C use mainly these chords.

## The pattern for every key

Because every major scale has the same formula, the order of chord types is **always the same**:

<Callout type="note" title="Major – minor – minor – Major – Major – minor – diminished">
**I – ii – iii – IV – V – vi – vii°**. **Upper-case** Roman numerals are major chords, **lower-case** are minor, and **°** means diminished. Memorise this line and you know the chords of every major key.
</Callout>

| Key | I | ii | iii | IV | V | vi | vii° |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C | C | Dm | Em | F | G | Am | B° |
| G | G | Am | Bm | C | D | Em | F♯° |
| D | D | Em | F♯m | G | A | Bm | C♯° |
| A | A | Bm | C♯m | D | E | F♯m | G♯° |
| F | F | Gm | Am | B♭ | C | Dm | E° |

## The three primary chords and the relative minor

- **I, IV and V** are the three major chords — the backbone of countless songs: <PlayChords chords="C F G C" />
- **vi** is the tonic chord of the relative minor (Am for C major) — add it and the song instantly gets a sadder colour: <PlayChords chords="Am F C G" />
- **I – V – vi – IV** is probably the most common progression in pop music: <PlayChords chords="C G Am F" />

Because you think in Roman numerals, you can play I – V – vi – IV in **any key**: in G it is G – D – Em – C, in D it is D – A – Bm – G. That is the fastest way to transpose.
