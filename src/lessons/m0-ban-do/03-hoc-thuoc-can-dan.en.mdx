In the previous lesson you learned how to **find** a note. This one trains you to find it **instantly**, without counting frets — like reading words, you just recognise them. It is what lets you play scales, build chords and improvise anywhere on the neck.

<OnlyFor instrument="guitar">

## Two zones on a string

A string has only **12 different notes**; from fret 12 on everything repeats an octave higher. So we split the neck into two zones:

- **Frets 0–11** (low zone)
- **Fret 12 and up** (high zone — up to the last fret of your guitar; set your fret count under Options, e.g. 20)

Within **one zone**, each note name has **exactly one position** on a string. So when you practise, the app gives you the note name and the zone to search (shaded on a small neck) — no other notation needed.

## Level 2: the whole string

Now the zone changes at random each time: look at the small neck to see whether to search the low or the high zone. Tip: a note in the high zone is its low-zone position **plus 12**, but try to recognise it straight away from the fret markers (frets 12, 15, 17…) rather than adding in your head.

## Several strings: octave shapes

In **Find them all**, the app gives a note name and you click **every** position of it from fret 0 to 12 on all six strings. There is no "add 12" trick here, because the strings are a 4th apart (strings 3–2 a major 3rd). You will gradually see the **octave shapes** from the previous lesson join up into a network across the neck.

## Going further

The **Note reflex** page (top bar) also has:

- **Note + string**: "F♯ — string 3", with the string chosen at random among the strings you enable.
- **In a position**: search only a 5-fret window (e.g. frets 5–9) — exactly how you think when improvising inside a scale box.
- **Advanced mode**: note names with octaves (open string 6 is E2, string 6 fret 12 is E3), and the **Exact pitch** drill: find every place the same pitch can be played.
- Running with the **metronome**, using your **real guitar through the mic** (the mic hears the octave but cannot tell strings apart), and a **blind-spot map** after each session: darker cells are where you were wrong or slow. Those positions come up more often in later sessions.

## Piano: see the key, know the note

On the piano each note has only **one** key per octave and every octave looks the same, so "learning the keyboard" is much quicker than learning a fretboard. The reflex drills in this lesson are for guitar. On the piano, practise naming notes quickly, aiming to answer within 2 seconds:
