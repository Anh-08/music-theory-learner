Everything in music theory — intervals, scales, chords — is described with note names. If you have to count frets or keys to know what you are playing, every later lesson will feel slow. This lesson helps you find notes **instantly**.

<OnlyFor instrument="guitar">

## Guitar: open strings and two anchor strings

The six open strings, from the lowest (string 6) to the highest (string 1): **E – A – D – G – B – E**.

The fastest way in is to know the **natural notes on strings 6 and 5** really well — that is where the roots of most barre chords and scale shapes live:

<Show notes="C D E F G A B" positions="6:0 6:1 6:3 6:5 6:7 6:8 6:10 6:12 5:0 5:2 5:3 5:5 5:7 5:8 5:10 5:12" play="none" caption="Natural notes on strings 6 and 5 — C is shown in red as a landmark" />

A few memory aids:

- The **fret markers** (frets 3, 5, 7, 9, 12) are landmarks: string 6 fret 3 = G, fret 5 = A, fret 7 = B; string 5 fret 3 = C, fret 5 = D, fret 7 = E.
- **Fret 12** is the open string an octave higher — from fret 12 on, everything repeats.
- Remember that E–F and B–C are only 1 fret apart: string 6 fret 0 (E) → fret 1 (F); string 5 fret 2 (B) → fret 3 (C).

## Octave shapes

Once you know one note, find the others with the same name using **octave shapes**:

- From string 6 or 5: **two strings over, two frets up**.
- From string 4 or 3: **two strings over, three frets up** (because the B string is tuned one fret "off").

## Piano: the black keys are your map

The black keys come in alternating **groups of 2** and **groups of 3**. That is the only landmark you need:

- **C** is the white key just **to the left of a group of 2 black keys**.
- **F** is the white key just **to the left of a group of 3 black keys**.
- Going up from C: C D E (around the group of 2), then F G A B (around the group of 3).

<Show notes="C D E F G A B" play="none" caption="The seven natural notes are the seven white keys — C is shown in red" />

**C4** — "middle C" — is the C closest to the middle of the keyboard (usually right under the brand name). Black keys take their names from the white keys next to them: the black key between C and D is **C♯ / D♭**.
