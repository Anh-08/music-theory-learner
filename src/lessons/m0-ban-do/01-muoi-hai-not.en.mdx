## Only 12 notes

Ballad, rock or jazz — Western music uses just **12 notes**, repeated at different heights. Seven of them are the **natural notes**, which you probably already know:

| Letter | C | D | E | F | G | A | B |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Solfège | Do | Re | Mi | Fa | Sol | La | Ti |

The other five sit *between* the natural notes. In this app we use letter names (C, D, E…), since that is how chord sheets are written.

## Half steps and whole steps

- A **half step** (semitone) is the smallest distance between two notes. On guitar: move **1 fret**. On piano: move to the **very next key** (black keys included).
- A **whole step** (tone) = 2 half steps. Guitar: 2 frets. Piano: skip one key.

Here are the 12 notes going up one half step at a time, ending on C again, higher:

<Callout type="note" title="Two special pairs: E–F and B–C">
**E and F**, and **B and C**, are only a **half step** apart — there is no note between them. On a piano you can see it straight away: no black key sits between these pairs. Every other pair of natural notes is a whole step apart.
</Callout>

## Sharps (♯) and flats (♭)

- **♯ (sharp)** raises a note by a half step: C♯ is the note just above C.
- **♭ (flat)** lowers a note by a half step: D♭ is the note just below D.

So C♯ and D♭ are **the same key, the same sound** — they are called **enharmonic** notes. When do we say C♯ and when D♭? That depends on the *key* of the song, which you will learn in Module 2. For now just remember: every black key has two names.

<Callout type="tip">
In theory there are also E♯ (= F) and C♭ (= B). They look odd, but they show up in keys with many sharps or flats. Don't worry about them yet.
</Callout>

## The octave: same name, higher

After 12 half steps you arrive at a note with the same name but twice the frequency — an **octave** higher. Two notes an octave apart sound so "alike" that we give them the same name: <Play notes="C4 C5" /> <Play notes="A3 A4" />

The number after a note name tells you the octave: **C4** is "middle C" on the piano and **C5** is one octave higher. On guitar, the open 6th string is **E2**.
