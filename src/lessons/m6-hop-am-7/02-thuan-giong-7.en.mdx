In Module 3 you built a triad on each degree. Now stack one more note — still using only notes from the scale:

| Degree | Notes (C major) | Chord | Roman numeral |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | C E G B | Cmaj7 | Imaj7 |
| 2 | D F A C | Dm7 | ii7 |
| 3 | E G B D | Em7 | iii7 |
| 4 | F A C E | Fmaj7 | IVmaj7 |
| 5 | G B D F | G7 | V7 |
| 6 | A C E G | Am7 | vi7 |
| 7 | B D F A | Bm7♭5 | viiø7 |

Listen in order: <PlayChords chords="Cmaj7 Dm7 Em7 Fmaj7 G7 Am7 Bm7b5 Cmaj7" seconds={1.2} />

The pattern for every major key: **maj7 – m7 – m7 – maj7 – 7 – m7 – m7♭5**.

<Callout type="tip" title="Only one dominant 7th">
In each major key only **degree V** is a dominant 7th chord. See G7 on a chord sheet → the song is almost certainly heading to C. The **Tools → Keys** page shows this table for every key.
</Callout>

## ii – V – I

The backbone of jazz: **Dm7 – G7 – Cmaj7**. Subdominant → dominant → tonic.

The magic is in the **voice leading**: the 7th of each chord moves down a half step to become the 3rd of the next:

- Dm7 → G7: **C** (7th of Dm7) falls to **B** (3rd of G7).
- G7 → Cmaj7: **F** (7th of G7) falls to **E** (3rd of Cmaj7).

Hear the lines: <Play notes="C5 B4 B4">C – B – B</Play> <Play notes="F4 F4 E4">F – F – E</Play>

## ii – V – i in a minor key

Minor keys have their own version: **iiø7 – V7 – i**. In A minor: **Bm7♭5 – E7 – Am**. The E7 (instead of Em7) comes from the harmonic minor, with the leading tone G♯ pulling strongly to A.
