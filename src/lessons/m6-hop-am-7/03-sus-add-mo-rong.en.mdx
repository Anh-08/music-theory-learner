## Suspended chords: sus2 and sus4

Replace the 3rd with a 2nd or a 4th and you get a **suspended** chord:

| Chord | Formula | Root D |
|---|---|---|
| Dsus2 | 1 – 2 – 5 | D E A |
| Dsus4 | 1 – 4 – 5 | D G A |

With no 3rd, a suspended chord is **neither major nor minor** — it sounds floating, expectant. The classic use: suspend, then **resolve to the triad**: <PlayChords chords="D Dsus4 D Dsus2 D" seconds={1} />

There is also **7sus4** (1 – 4 – 5 – ♭7) — a "suspended" dominant, very often used before V7 or in place of it.

## Added notes: add9 and 6

- **add9** (1 – 3 – 5 – 9): adds the 9th (= the 2nd an octave up) without a 7th. Clear and modern: <PlayChords chords="C Cadd9 C" seconds={1.3} />
- **6** (1 – 3 – 5 – 6) and **m6** (1 – ♭3 – 5 – 6): sweet, slightly vintage.

<Callout type="note" title="Same notes, different names">
C6 = C E G A. Am7 = A C E G. **The same four notes!** Whether it is C6 or Am7 depends on which note is in the bass and what role the chord plays in the progression.
</Callout>

## Extensions: 9, 11, 13

Keep stacking thirds on a 7th chord and you get **extensions** — degrees 2, 4 and 6, but an octave higher:

| Note | Equals | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 9 | 2 (an octave up) | C9 = C E G B♭ **D** |
| 11 | 4 | Cm11 = C E♭ G B♭ D **F** |
| 13 | 6 | C13 = C E G B♭ D (F) **A** |

Listen: <PlayChords chords="C9 Cmaj9 Cm9" seconds={1.6} />

In practice players **leave notes out**: drop the 5th (it barely affects the colour), and sometimes even the root (the bass player covers it). Keep the **3rd, the 7th and the extension** — those are the colour notes.

<Callout type="tip" title="Careful with the 11 on major chords">
The 11th (= 4) is only a half step above the major 3rd (F over the E of Cmaj7), so it clashes badly. On major chords players use the **♯11** (F♯) instead — the Lydian colour you will meet in Module 8.
</Callout>
