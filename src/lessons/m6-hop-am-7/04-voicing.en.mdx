A seventh chord has 4 notes — and guitar and piano offer many ways to arrange them. This lesson gives you voicings you can **use right away** to accompany songs with seventh chords.

<OnlyFor instrument="guitar">

## Movable shapes, root on string 5

Root on string 5 (e.g. C at fret 3), using strings 5 – 4 – 3 – 2:

<Show root="C" chord="maj7" shape="x3545x" labels="interval" caption="Cmaj7: x 3 5 4 5 x" />

<Show root="C" chord="7" shape="x3535x" labels="interval" caption="C7: x 3 5 3 5 x" />

<Show root="C" chord="m7" shape="x3534x" labels="interval" caption="Cm7: x 3 5 3 4 x" />

<Show root="C" chord="m7b5" shape="x3434x" labels="interval" caption="Cm7♭5: x 3 4 3 4 x" />

With these 8 shapes you can play **every** maj7, 7, m7 and m7♭5 chord in every key — all you need is the note names on strings 6 and 5.

## ii – V – I without jumping around

Combine the two groups: Dm7 (root on string 5, fret 5) → G7 (root on string 6, fret 3) → Cmaj7 (root on string 5, fret 3). Your hand stays around frets 3 – 5.

## Left hand root, right hand colour

The simplest approach: the **left hand** plays the root (or root + 7th), the **right hand** plays the 3rd – 5th – 7th (or 3rd – 7th – 9th).

<Show notes="C3 E4 G4 B4" labels="interval" play="chord" caption="Cmaj7: left hand C, right hand E – G – B" />

## A guide-tone line for ii – V – I

The **3rd and 7th** alone are enough to hear the chord quality. The right hand holds these two notes, and each chord change moves by a half step or not at all:

<Show notes="G2 B3 F4" labels="note" play="chord" caption="G7: C falls to B, F stays (now B is the 3rd, F the 7th)" />
