Time to really play. The **minor pentatonic** is the most used scale in rock, blues and pop solos — and the easiest way to start improvising.

## Five notes

Take the natural minor scale and drop degrees 2 and 6; five notes remain:

| | 1 | ♭3 | 4 | 5 | ♭7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A minor pentatonic | A | C | D | E | G |

<Show root="A" scale="minor pentatonic" labels="interval" />

**Why "can't get wrong"?** Seven-note scales contain pairs of notes a half step apart (like E – F, B – C) — play them clumsily and they clash. The pentatonic has **no half steps at all**, so almost any note sounds fine over the chords of the key.

<OnlyFor instrument="guitar">

## Box 1 — the most important guitar shape

Put your index finger at fret 5 (A minor pentatonic). Each string has exactly 2 notes; the index finger takes fret 5, the ring / little finger take frets 7 – 8:

<Show root="A" scale="minor pentatonic" positions="6:5 6:8 5:5 5:7 4:5 4:7 3:5 3:7 2:5 2:8 1:5 1:8" frets={15} labels="interval" caption="Box 1, A minor pentatonic (frets 5–8). Red = A, the 'home' note" />

This is a **movable** shape: the root sits under your index finger on string 6. Slide it up to fret 7 for B minor pentatonic, down to fret 0 / 12 for E minor pentatonic.

</OnlyFor>

<OnlyFor instrument="piano">

## On the keyboard

A minor pentatonic uses only white keys: A – C – D – E – G. Put the right-hand thumb on A: 1 (A) – 2 (C) – 3 (D) – 4 (E) – then pass the thumb under to G or shift the hand.

<Show notes="A3 C4 D4 E4 G4 A4 C5 D5 E5 G5 A5" labels="interval" caption="A minor pentatonic, two octaves" />

Tip: let the left hand hold an A (or an Am chord) as a base while the right hand improvises.

</OnlyFor>

## Your first improvisation

Start the backing track below (Am – Dm). Don't try to play a lot — follow these steps:

1. **Use only 3 notes**: A, C and D. Play short phrases of 3 – 4 notes.
2. **Call and response**: play a phrase for 2 bars, then **stay silent** for 2 bars. Silence is music too.
3. **End your phrases on A** (the red note). Hear how it "comes home".
4. **Repeat your own ideas**: play the phrase you just played again, changing one note or the rhythm.
5. Once you feel comfortable, open up to all 5 notes.

<Jam progression="Am*2 Dm*2" songKey="Am" groove="rock" tempo={88} scale="minor pentatonic" />

<Callout type="tip" title="Watch the instrument while the music plays">
The **blue and red** notes belong to the chord that is sounding — they sound the most "right", especially on beat 1. The **grey** notes are the rest of the scale, for moving between them. When the chord changes to Dm, D becomes the red note: try ending a phrase on D at that moment.
</Callout>
