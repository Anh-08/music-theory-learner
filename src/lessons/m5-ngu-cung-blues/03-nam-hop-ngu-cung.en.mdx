One box is enough to start, but sooner or later you will want to play higher, lower and slide along the instrument. This lesson spreads the pentatonic across your whole instrument.

<OnlyFor instrument="guitar">

## Five boxes

The five notes of A minor pentatonic repeat all over the neck. Split them into 5 zones and you get **5 boxes**; each box shares an "edge" with the next one. The full picture:

<Callout type="tip" title="Learning the boxes without overload">
Don't learn all 5 boxes at once. Suggested order: **box 1** (know it by heart) → **box 2** (connect upwards) → **box 5** (connect downwards). These three already cover frets 2 – 10. Always keep an eye on **where the root (red) is** in each box — it is your anchor for ending phrases.
</Callout>

## Connecting boxes: sliding along the neck

Neighbouring boxes share notes along their edge. For example: play up through box 1, and when you reach string 3 fret 7 (D), slide up to fret 9 (E) — you are now in box 2. Play over the backing track below and try sliding back and forth between two boxes.

## The pentatonic in every key

The piano has no "boxes", but it does have familiar hand shapes:

- **The black-key pentatonic**: the five black keys are exactly **G♭ major pentatonic** (G♭ A♭ B♭ D♭ E♭) — which is also **E♭ minor pentatonic**. Run your hand over the black keys and you instantly hear the pentatonic sound, and you can hardly go wrong!

<Show notes="Eb4 Gb4 Ab4 Bb4 Db5 Eb5" labels="interval" caption="E♭ minor pentatonic — all black keys" />

- **Three white-key keys**: A minor pentatonic (A C D E G), D minor pentatonic (D F G A C) and E minor pentatonic (E G A B D) use only white keys.

<Show notes="E4 G4 A4 B4 D5 E5" labels="interval" caption="E minor pentatonic" />

<Show notes="D4 F4 G4 A4 C5 D5" labels="interval" caption="D minor pentatonic" />

- **Other keys**: build them with the formula 1 – ♭3 – 4 – 5 – ♭7 (minor pentatonic) or 1 – 2 – 3 – 5 – 6 (major pentatonic). Practise one new key a week around the circle of fifths.

</OnlyFor>
