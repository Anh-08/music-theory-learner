## Five bright notes

The **major pentatonic** is the major scale without degrees **4** and **7** — exactly the two most "tense" notes you met in Module 2:

| | 1 | 2 | 3 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C major pentatonic | C | D | E | G | A |

<Show root="C" scale="major pentatonic" labels="interval" />

It sounds happy, open and clear — perfect for pop, country and many folk melodies (including Vietnamese folk songs, which rely heavily on pentatonic scales).

## The secret: the same notes as the minor pentatonic

Compare: C major pentatonic = **C D E G A**. A minor pentatonic = **A C D E G**. The same five notes!

Just as C major and A minor are relative keys (Module 2), **the major pentatonic of X shares its notes with the minor pentatonic starting on degree 6 of X** (three half steps below X). What differs is **which note is "home"**:

- Over a progression in C major → think "C is home", end phrases on C, E, G.
- Over a progression in A minor → think "A is home", end phrases on A, C, E.

<OnlyFor instrument="guitar">

So box 1 of A minor pentatonic (fret 5) is also a C major pentatonic shape — you just change the home note. Look at the interval labels counted from C:

<Show root="C" scale="major pentatonic" positions="6:5 6:8 5:5 5:7 4:5 4:7 3:5 3:7 2:5 2:8 1:5 1:8" frets={15} labels="interval" caption="The same box 1 shape at fret 5, but now the red note is C" />

Quick trick: want G major pentatonic? Take the minor pentatonic box 1 shape and put your index finger on **E** (three frets below G) — string 6 fret 12 (or fret 0).

</OnlyFor>

## Playing over a major-key progression

Start I – V – vi – IV in C. Improvise with C major pentatonic, and try to **end phrases on a note of the current chord** (the blue / red notes on the instrument).

<Callout type="tip" title="Same notes, different feeling">
Try playing exactly the phrases you used over Am – Dm in the previous lesson. Over a C major backing they naturally sound brighter — because the chords change which note feels like home. Harmony decides the colour of a melody.
