## Same notes, different "home"

Take the seven white keys (the notes of C major). Treat **C** as home and you get the major scale. Treat **A** as home and you get the natural minor (Module 2). What about the other five notes? Each one as "home" gives a **mode**:

| Starting on | Mode | Also known as |
|---|---|---|
| C | Ionian | = the major scale |
| D | Dorian | |
| E | Phrygian | |
| F | Lydian | |
| G | Mixolydian | |
| A | Aeolian | = natural minor |
| B | Locrian | |

Listen to D Dorian — all white keys, but home is D: <Play notes="D4 E4 F4 G4 A4 B4 C5 D5">D Dorian</Play>

## Hearing them from the same root

The view above is easy to understand but hard to **hear**. A better way: compare modes **on the same root**, each differing from the major scale in a few notes:

| Mode | Compared with major | Characteristic note | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lydian | ♯4 | ♯4 | Brightest, floating, dreamy |
| Ionian | — | | Bright, stable |
| Mixolydian | ♭7 | ♭7 | Major but "earthy" — rock, blues |
| Dorian | ♭3 ♭7 | 6 (major) | Minor but soulful — funk, soul |
| Aeolian | ♭3 ♭6 ♭7 | ♭6 | Sad, melancholic |
| Phrygian | ♭2 ♭3 ♭6 ♭7 | ♭2 | Spanish, dramatic, metal |
| Locrian | ♭2 ♭3 ♭5 ♭6 ♭7 | ♭5 | Darkest, unstable |

The table runs **from brightest to darkest** — each step down lowers just one more note.

<Callout type="tip" title="Think in characteristic notes">
When playing, don't think of modes as "the major scale starting on another degree". Think **"a familiar major / minor scale with one note changed"**. Dorian = natural minor with a raised 6th. Mixolydian = major with a lowered 7th. Lydian = major with a raised 4th. Phrygian = natural minor with a lowered 2nd.
</Callout>
