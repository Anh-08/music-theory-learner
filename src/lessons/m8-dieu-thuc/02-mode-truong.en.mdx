Three modes have a **major 3rd** (used over major chords): Ionian, Lydian and Mixolydian. The best way to feel a mode's colour is to play over a **vamp** — one or two chords repeated forever — so your ear locks onto the tonic.

## Ionian — the major scale

You know it well. Stable and bright. The note to "be careful with" is degree 4 (it clashes with the 3rd of the tonic chord).

## Lydian — major with a ♯4

Raise degree 4 by a half step: no more clash with the 3rd, and instead a **floating, dreamy** feel — very common in film music. *The Simpsons* theme is built on the Lydian colour.

The classic Lydian vamp: **I – II** (C – D). The D major chord contains F♯ — the ♯4 of C.

## Mixolydian — major with a ♭7

Lower degree 7: the leading tone disappears and the sound becomes **relaxed and "earthy"** — the colour of rock, blues, country and Celtic music. Mixolydian fits dominant 7th chords (1 3 5 ♭7) perfectly.

The classic Mixolydian vamp: **I – ♭VII** (G – F) — as in *Sweet Home Alabama* and countless rock songs.

<Callout type="tip" title="Quick thinking">
G Mixolydian = the notes of **C major**, with G as home. A Lydian = the notes of **E major**, with A as home. But while playing, think "G major with a lowered 7th" — your ear and hands will be faster.
</Callout>
