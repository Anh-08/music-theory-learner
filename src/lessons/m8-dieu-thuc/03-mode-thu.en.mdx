Four modes have a **minor 3rd**, from brightest to darkest: Dorian → Aeolian → Phrygian → Locrian.

## Dorian — minor with a major 6th

Natural minor with degree 6 raised a half step. The result: still sad, but **soulful rather than gloomy** — the colour of funk, soul and jazz, and of *Oye Como Va* (Santana) and *Scarborough Fair*.

The classic Dorian vamp: **i – IV** (Am7 – D7). The major IV chord (D) contains F♯ — the major 6th of A.

## Aeolian — natural minor

The minor scale you know from Module 2. The **♭6** gives a sad, melancholic colour. A common progression: i – ♭VI – ♭VII (Am – F – G).

<Jam progression="Am F G Am" songKey="Am" groove="ballad" scale="aeolian" />

## Phrygian — minor with a ♭2

Lower degree 2 as well: the **♭2** sits only a half step above the tonic, giving a **Spanish, flamenco** colour that metal players love too.

The Phrygian vamp: **i – ♭II** (Em – F).

## Locrian — the darkest mode

It has both ♭2 and **♭5** — its tonic chord is diminished, so it is very hard to feel as "home". It is rarely used as the mode of a whole song; mostly it is used over **m7♭5 chords** (next lesson).
