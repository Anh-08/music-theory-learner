## 6ths and 7ths

| Interval | Half steps | From C | Listen |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minor 6th (♭6) | 8 | C – A♭ | <Play notes="C4 Ab4" /> |
| Major 6th (6) | 9 | C – A | <Play notes="C4 A4" /> |
| Minor 7th (♭7) | 10 | C – B♭ | <Play notes="C4 Bb4" /> |
| Major 7th (7) | 11 | C – B | <Play notes="C4 B4" /> |

The minor 7th is the "spice" of 7th chords (C7, Am7); the major 7th gives maj7 chords their dreamy colour.

## All 12 intervals

Here is every interval within an octave — a table you will use for the rest of the course:

| Half steps | Name | Symbol | From C |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Unison | 1 | C |
| 1 | Minor 2nd | ♭2 | D♭ |
| 2 | Major 2nd | 2 | D |
| 3 | Minor 3rd | ♭3 | E♭ |
| 4 | Major 3rd | 3 | E |
| 5 | Perfect 4th | 4 | F |
| 6 | Augmented 4th / diminished 5th (tritone) | ♯4 / ♭5 | F♯ / G♭ |
| 7 | Perfect 5th | 5 | G |
| 8 | Minor 6th | ♭6 | A♭ |
| 9 | Major 6th | 6 | A |
| 10 | Minor 7th | ♭7 | B♭ |
| 11 | Major 7th | 7 | B |
| 12 | Perfect octave | 8 | C |

The **symbol** column is exactly how chord and scale formulas are written: a minor chord is "1 – ♭3 – 5", the minor pentatonic is "1 – ♭3 – 4 – 5 – ♭7".

## The inversion trick

Move the top note of an interval below the bottom one (or vice versa) and you get the **inverted interval**:

- The numbers always add up to **9**: 3 ↔ 6, 2 ↔ 7, 4 ↔ 5.
- The quality flips: **major ↔ minor**, **augmented ↔ diminished**, **perfect stays perfect**.

Example: C up to A is a major 6th ⟷ A up to C is a minor 3rd. Want to find a major 6th *above* a note quickly? Find the minor 3rd *below* it and go up an octave.
