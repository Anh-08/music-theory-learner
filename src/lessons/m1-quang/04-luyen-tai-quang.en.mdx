Knowing interval names on paper is not enough. When you improvise, you need to *hear* a melody in your head and instantly know which interval it is so you can find it on your instrument. This lesson gets you started.

## Link each interval to a song

The quickest way to remember the sound of an interval is to link it to the **first two notes** of a familiar melody:

| Interval (ascending) | Listen | Melody to remember |
|---|---|---|
| Minor 2nd | <Play notes="E4 F4" /> | The *Jaws* theme |
| Major 2nd | <Play notes="C4 D4" /> | *Happy Birthday* — from the 2nd note to the 3rd |
| Minor 3rd | <Play notes="A4 C5" /> | *Greensleeves*; the *Smoke on the Water* riff |
| Major 3rd | <Play notes="C4 E4" /> | *When the Saints Go Marching In* |
| Perfect 4th | <Play notes="D4 G4" /> | *Amazing Grace*; *Here Comes the Bride* |
| Tritone | <Play notes="C4 F#4" /> | *The Simpsons* theme |
| Perfect 5th | <Play notes="C4 G4" /> | *Twinkle Twinkle Little Star*; the *Star Wars* theme |
| Minor 6th | <Play notes="E4 C5" /> | *The Entertainer* (after the two pickup notes) |
| Major 6th | <Play notes="G4 E5" /> | *My Bonnie Lies over the Ocean* |
| Minor 7th | <Play notes="C4 Bb4" /> | *Somewhere* (West Side Story) |
| Major 7th | <Play notes="C4 B4" /> | The chorus of *Take On Me* |
| Octave | <Play notes="C4 C5" /> | *Somewhere Over the Rainbow* |

<Callout type="tip" title="Use your own songs">
Songs you know by heart always beat the table above. Whenever you play a familiar song, try to name the interval between the first two notes of a phrase and write it down.
</Callout>

<Callout type="practice" title="Pick up your instrument">
Five minutes a day: play any note, **sing** an interval (for example a perfect 5th), and only then play that note to check. Singing first and playing second is the fastest way to connect your ear to your hands.
</Callout>
