An **interval** is the distance between two notes. Chords are intervals stacked on top of each other, melodies are intervals one after another — so understanding intervals means understanding the raw material of music.

Two notes can sound **one after the other** (a melodic interval) or **together** (a harmonic interval): <Play notes="C4 E4" as="both">C and E</Play>

## An interval name has two parts

An interval such as a "**major 3rd**" has:

1. **A number** — count the *letter names*, including the first and last. C → E: C, D, E = **3** letters → a 3rd.
2. **A quality** — count the *half steps* to tell whether it is major, minor, perfect, augmented or diminished.

For 2nds and 3rds:

| Interval | Half steps | From C | Listen |
|---|---|---|---|
| Minor 2nd (♭2) | 1 | C – D♭ | <Play notes="C4 Db4" /> |
| Major 2nd (2) | 2 | C – D | <Play notes="C4 D4" /> |
| Minor 3rd (♭3) | 3 | C – E♭ | <Play notes="C4 Eb4" /> |
| Major 3rd (3) | 4 | C – E | <Play notes="C4 E4" /> |

A minor 3rd sounds "sad", a major 3rd sounds "bright" — these two intervals decide whether a chord is major or minor (Module 3).

<Callout type="note" title="Why count letters?">
C – D♯ and C – E♭ are both 3 half steps, the same sound. But C → D♯ spans only 2 letters (C, D), so it is an **augmented 2nd**, while C → E♭ spans 3 letters and is a **minor 3rd**. The correct name tells you what role the note plays — that matters when reading chords and scales. In practice you will meet the minor 3rd far more often.
</Callout>

## Seeing intervals on your instrument

<OnlyFor instrument="guitar">

On guitar, each interval has a fixed **shape** — move it anywhere and it stays the same:

- **Major 2nd:** same string, up 2 frets. **Minor 2nd:** same string, up 1 fret.
- **Major 3rd:** next string up, **back 1 fret**.
- **Minor 3rd:** next string up, **back 2 frets**.

<Show notes="C E" positions="5:3 4:2" labels="interval" caption="Major 3rd: C (string 5 fret 3) → E (string 4 fret 2)" />

<Show notes="C Eb" positions="5:3 4:1" labels="interval" caption="Minor 3rd: C (string 5 fret 3) → E♭ (string 4 fret 1)" />

The one exception is going from **string 3 (G) to string 2 (B)**: every shape **moves up 1 fret** (a major 3rd is the same fret, a minor 3rd is back 1 fret). You will meet this rule again and again.

</OnlyFor>

<OnlyFor instrument="piano">

On piano, just count keys (white and black): a major 3rd is the 4th key above the starting note, a minor 3rd is the 3rd.

<Show notes="C4 E4" labels="interval" play="chord" caption="Major 3rd: C – E (4 half steps)" />

<Show notes="D4 F4" labels="interval" play="chord" caption="Minor 3rd: D – F (3 half steps) — both white keys, but a different quality" />

Don't just look for "skip one white key": C–E and D–F both skip one white key, but C–E is a major 3rd and D–F is a minor 3rd.

</OnlyFor>
