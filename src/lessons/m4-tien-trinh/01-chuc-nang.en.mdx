In Module 3 you met the 7 diatonic chords. But they are not equal: each chord plays a **role** in the story of a song. Understanding chord roles is understanding why a progression sounds "right" — and lets you guess the next chord when hearing a new song.

## The three functions

| Function | Feeling | Chords (major key) | In C major |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tonic** (T) | Home, settled | **I**, vi, iii | **C**, Am, Em |
| **Subdominant** (S) | Leaving home, opening up | **IV**, ii | **F**, Dm |
| **Dominant** (D) | Tense, wants to go home | **V**, vii° | **G**, B° |

Chords with the same function share many notes: Am (A C E) shares C and E with C (C E G) — so Am can "stand in" for C. Dm (D F A) shares F and A with F (F A C).

The basic journey of harmony is **T → S → D → T** — home, out, tension, home: <PlayChords chords="C F G C" />

## Why does the dominant want to go to the tonic?

The G chord (G – B – D) contains **B — the leading tone** of C. B is only a half step from C, so it is pulled up to C very strongly. Add a 7th to get **G7** (G – B – D – F) and F also wants to fall to E — and B and F form a **tritone**, the most tense interval. That tension is fully released when you land on C: <PlayChords chords="G7 C" seconds={1.8} />

On top of that, the root moves from G down to C — **down a fifth**, the strongest motion around the circle of fifths.

## Cadences: how phrases end

The way a phrase ends creates different feelings:

| Cadence | Progression | Feeling | Listen |
|---|---|---|---|
| Authentic | V → I | A full stop. Phrase over. | <PlayChords chords="Dm G C" /> |
| Plagal | IV → I | Gentle, like "A-men" | <PlayChords chords="C F C" /> |
| Half | … → V | A comma, an unanswered question | <PlayChords chords="C Am F G" /> |
| Deceptive | V → vi | You expect home but turn somewhere sad | <PlayChords chords="F G Am" /> |

<Callout type="tip" title="Use it when listening">
Next time you listen to a song, notice **the end of each phrase**: which chord does the line end on? Mid-song phrases often end on V (a half cadence, so the song can continue), and the final phrase ends on I. Hearing this means you have started "hearing harmony".
</Callout>
