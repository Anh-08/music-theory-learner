Most popular songs reuse a small group of progressions. Once you recognise "ah, this is I – V – vi – IV", you can accompany it straight away in any key — and know immediately which scale to improvise with.

## I – IV – V

The three major chords of the key. The foundation of rock and roll, folk, country and many children's songs. Usually as I – IV – V – I or I – IV – I – V.

<Jam progression="G C D C" songKey="G" groove="rock" tempo={110} />

## I – V – vi – IV

Probably the most common progression in modern pop — *Let It Be* (The Beatles) is a famous example. Start at home, go up to the dominant, turn to the minor chord, then open up on IV.

<Jam progression="C G Am F" songKey="C" groove="pop" />

Start it from a different place and you get **vi – IV – I – V** — the same 4 chords, but starting on the minor chord makes it sadder and more yearning (*Despacito*, *Zombie* by The Cranberries).

<Jam progression="Am F C G" songKey="C" groove="pop" />

## I – vi – IV – V

The "fifties progression" — *Stand By Me* is the classic example. In C it is C – Am – F – G, one of the first progressions many guitarists learn.

<Jam progression="C Am F G" songKey="C" groove="ballad" />

## The Canon progression

From Pachelbel's *Canon in D*: **I – V – vi – iii – IV – I – IV – V**. The bass steps down gradually — countless ballads use this progression or part of it.

<Jam progression="D A Bm F#m G D G A" songKey="D" groove="ballad" />

## ii – V – I

The backbone of jazz: subdominant (ii) → dominant (V) → tonic (I). Usually with 7th chords: Dm7 – G7 – Cmaj7. You will study 7th chords properly in Module 6, but listening ahead does no harm:

<Jam progression="Dm7 G7 Cmaj7*2" songKey="C" groove="bossa" />

## A minor-key progression: i – ♭VII – ♭VI – V

Known as the **Andalusian cadence** — it sounds very "Spanish" (*Hit the Road Jack*). In A minor: Am – G – F – E. The E major chord (instead of Em) creates the leading tone G♯, which pulls strongly back to A.

<Jam progression="Am G F E" songKey="Am" groove="rock" tempo={100} />

<Callout type="note" title="Don't worry about 'copying' a progression">
Two songs with the same progression can still be completely different thanks to melody, rhythm and arrangement. A progression is only the foundation — which is good news: learn a few progressions and you already have the foundation for a lot of songs.
</Callout>
