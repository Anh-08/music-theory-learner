This is a skill you will use every day: take a chord sheet, understand it, and play it in the key you want.

## Step 1 — Find the key

A few clues, taken together:

1. **The first and last chords** of a song are usually the tonic chord (I or i).
2. **All the chords belong to one key**: the three major chords I, IV and V of a key sit next to each other on the circle of fifths, with the tonic **in the middle**. For example F, C, G → the key of C (F = IV, G = V).
3. **Frequent sharps or flats** reveal the key signature: lots of F♯m, Bm, D → probably D or G major.
4. **Major or minor?** If the song starts and ends on a minor chord (like Am), the relative minor (A minor) is usually the better answer.

The Jam Room has a built-in **key detector**: type a progression and it tells you the key and the Roman numeral of each chord. Use it to check your analysis — but make your own guess first!
</Callout>

## Step 2 — Write the Roman numerals

Once you have the key, convert each chord to a degree. For example **E – B – C♯m – A** in E major (E F♯ G♯ A B C♯ D♯):

| Chord | E | B | C♯m | A |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Degree | I | V | vi | IV |

It is the familiar I – V – vi – IV again!

## Step 3 — Transpose

With Roman numerals, transposing is just **swapping notes**: I – V – vi – IV in C is **C – G – Am – F**. In G it is **G – D – Em – C**.

Another way: move every chord up or down by the same number of half steps. E → C is down 4 half steps, so B → G, C♯m → Am, A → F.

<OnlyFor instrument="guitar">

## The capo: changing key without changing shapes

A **capo** at fret *n* raises everything by *n* half steps. You keep the shapes you know while the sound moves up:

| Chord shapes from the key of | Capo fret 2 | Capo fret 3 | Capo fret 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| G (G C D Em) | A | B♭ | B |
| C (C F G Am) | D | E♭ | E |
| D (D G A Bm) | E | F | F♯ |

For example, a song in E major that you want to play with easy shapes: use C-key shapes with a capo at fret 4, or D-key shapes with a capo at fret 2.

</OnlyFor>

<Callout type="note" title="Choosing a comfortable key">
On piano, keys with few black keys (C, G, F, Am) are often easier to read when starting out, but practise flat keys like E♭ and A♭ too — they are very common in pop and jazz, and often sit quite comfortably under the hand.
</Callout>
