Right notes with dull rhythm still make a bland solo. On the other hand, just a few notes with a great rhythm can become a memorable phrase. This lesson gives you tools to **tell a story** when you improvise.

## Motif: one small idea, used many times

A **motif** is a short musical idea (2 – 5 notes) with a clear shape and rhythm. Listeners remember the motif, so every time it returns (even slightly changed), the story feels coherent.

An example motif: <Play notes="E4 G4 A4 G4">E – G – A – G</Play>

## Five ways to vary a motif

1. **Repeat it exactly**: simple but effective.
2. **Move it with the chords (sequence)**: keep the shape, shift it up or down to fit the new chord: <Play notes="E4 G4 A4 G4 F4 A4 B4 A4">E G A G → F A B A</Play>
3. **Change the rhythm**: keep the notes, change the lengths — stretch the last note, or start off the beat.
4. **Add or remove notes**: add an approach note, or trim it to 2 notes.
5. **Invert the direction**: up becomes down: <Play notes="E4 C4 B3 C4">E – C – B – C</Play>

## Rhythm in a solo

- **Don't always start on beat 1**: starting on the "& of 1" or on beat 2 sounds more natural, like speaking.
- **Silence is an answer**: play 2 bars, rest 2 bars.
- **Mix note lengths**: a few quick notes, then one long held note.
- **Repeat a rhythm** with different notes — a rhythm can be a motif too.
