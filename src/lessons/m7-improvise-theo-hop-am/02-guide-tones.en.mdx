Of the four chord tones, two matter most: the **3rd** (major or minor?) and the **7th** (maj7, 7 or m7?). They are called **guide tones**. Hearing just these two notes, your ear already knows which chord it is.

## Guide tones in ii – V – I

| Chord | 3rd | 7th |
|---|---|---|
| Dm7 | F | C |
| G7 | B | F |
| Cmaj7 | E | B |

Notice: when the chord changes, each guide tone **stays put or moves a half step**:

- Line 1: **C → B → B** (7th of Dm7 → 3rd of G7 → 7th of Cmaj7)
- Line 2: **F → F → E** (3rd of Dm7 → 7th of G7 → 3rd of Cmaj7)

## Soloing with guide tones

Exercise: over the backing track, play **one guide tone on beat 1 of each chord**, choosing the one closest to your previous note. You will find the line "flows" with the harmony by itself — this is how jazz musicians build the skeleton of a melody before decorating it.

<Callout type="note" title="An A7 at the end?">
A7 is not in the key of C — it is a **secondary dominant** leading to Dm7 (Module 9). The guide tones of A7 are C♯ and G: C♯ rises to D and G falls to F — exactly the two guide tones of Dm7!
</Callout>
