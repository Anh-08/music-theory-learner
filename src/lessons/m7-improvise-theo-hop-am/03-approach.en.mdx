You know your **target** notes (chord tones). This lesson shows how to **arrive** at them beautifully — deliberately using notes *outside* the scale.

## Three ways to approach a target

Say the target is **E** (the 3rd of C), landing on beat 1:

| Approach | Example | Listen |
|---|---|---|
| Half step below (chromatic) | D♯ → **E** | <Play notes="Eb4 E4" /> |
| Scale note above | F → **E** | <Play notes="F4 E4" /> |
| Enclosure: above then below | F → D♯ → **E** | <Play notes="F4 Eb4 E4" /> |

D♯ is not in C major — but because it **immediately resolves** to E on a strong beat, the ear hears it as a bit of "spice", not a wrong note.

<Callout type="tip" title="The rhythm rule">
Put approach notes on **weak beats / off-beats** and the target on the **strong beat**. Reverse it (an outside note on the strong beat) and it sounds like a mistake.
</Callout>

## Connecting chords with approach notes

From C to Am: end a phrase on E over C, then approach **A** (root of Am) from G♯: <Play notes="E4 D4 C4 G#3 A3">… C – G♯ – A</Play>

From Dm to G: approach **B** (3rd of G) with the enclosure C – A♯ – B: <Play notes="D4 F4 A4 C5 A#4 B4">Dm … C – A♯ – B</Play>
