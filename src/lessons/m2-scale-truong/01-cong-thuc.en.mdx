A **scale** is a set of notes in order of pitch, starting from a note called the **tonic**. A scale tells you *which notes "belong" to the music*. When you improvise, the scale is your set of safe notes.

The most important scale is the **major scale** — the foundation of almost every idea that follows.

## The formula

Go up from C using only the natural notes and you get the C major scale: <Play notes="C4 D4 E4 F4 G4 A4 B4 C5" />

The distances between neighbouring notes are **not all the same**:

| C → D | D → E | E → F | F → G | G → A | A → B | B → C |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 | ½ | 1 | 1 | 1 | ½ |

The major scale formula: **1 – 1 – ½ – 1 – 1 – 1 – ½** (whole – whole – half – whole – whole – whole – half, often written W W H W W W H).

## Building scales from other notes

Apply the formula to **G**: G → A (1) → B (1) → C (½) → D (1) → E (1) → **F♯** (1) → G (½).

E → F is only a half step, but the formula needs a whole step — so F has to be raised to **F♯**.

<Show root="G" scale="major" labels="note" />

Apply it to **F**: F → G → A → **B♭** → C → D → E → F. A → B is a whole step, but the formula needs a half step — so B has to be lowered to **B♭**.

<Show root="F" scale="major" labels="note" />

<Callout type="note" title="Every letter appears exactly once">
Why B♭ and not A♯? Because a 7-note scale must use all 7 letters A–G, **each exactly once**. F G A **A♯** C D E would have two A's and no B. This rule keeps note names in step with degree numbers — very handy when working out intervals and chords.
</Callout>
