Every major scale has a fixed number of sharps or flats — called the **key signature** of that key. Instead of memorising 12 unrelated keys, there is a beautiful pattern that makes them easy to remember.

## Up a fifth: one more sharp

Starting from C major (no sharps or flats), each time you go up a **perfect 5th**, the new key has **one more sharp**:

| Key | ♯ | Sharp notes |
|---|---|---|
| C | 0 | — |
| G | 1 | F♯ |
| D | 2 | F♯ C♯ |
| A | 3 | F♯ C♯ G♯ |
| E | 4 | F♯ C♯ G♯ D♯ |
| B | 5 | F♯ C♯ G♯ D♯ A♯ |
| F♯ | 6 | F♯ C♯ G♯ D♯ A♯ E♯ |

Two interesting things:

- The new sharp is always **degree 7 (the leading tone)** of the new key: G major adds F♯, D major adds C♯.
- The order of sharps — **F C G D A E B** — is itself a chain of 5ths!

## Up a fourth: one more flat

Going the other way (up a **perfect 4th**, i.e. down a 5th), each key gains **one more flat**:

| Key | ♭ | Flat notes |
|---|---|---|
| F | 1 | B♭ |
| B♭ | 2 | B♭ E♭ |
| E♭ | 3 | B♭ E♭ A♭ |
| A♭ | 4 | B♭ E♭ A♭ D♭ |
| D♭ | 5 | B♭ E♭ A♭ D♭ G♭ |
| G♭ | 6 | B♭ E♭ A♭ D♭ G♭ C♭ |

The order of flats — **B E A D G C F** — is exactly the order of sharps backwards. A handy trick: the name of a flat key is the **second-to-last flat** (a key with B♭ E♭ A♭ → E♭ major).

## The circle of fifths

Join both directions and you get a closed loop — the **circle of fifths**. At the bottom, F♯ major (6♯) and G♭ major (6♭) are the same sound, just spelled differently.

## Relative minor keys

The inner ring shows the **relative minor keys**. Every major key has a minor key that **uses exactly the same notes**, starting on **degree 6**:

- C major: C D E F G A B → A minor: A B C D E F G (same notes, different "home").
- G major ↔ E minor, F major ↔ D minor…

<Callout type="tip" title="What is the circle of fifths for?">
- **Guessing a song's key**: a chord sheet full of F♯ and C♯ → most likely D major or B minor.
- **Neighbouring keys are "relatives"**: they share 6 of 7 notes and move into each other smoothly.
- **Chord progressions**: chords moving anticlockwise (like D → G → C) are the strongest motion in harmony — you will see this again in Module 4.
</Callout>
