Understanding the formula is one thing; playing the scale fluently is another. This lesson gives you **shapes** so your hands can run on autopilot while your mind listens to the music.

<OnlyFor instrument="guitar">

## One-octave shape, root on string 6

This shape starts with the root on string 6. Put your **2nd finger** on the root. For example, G major at fret 3:

<Show root="G" scale="major" positions="6:3 6:5 5:2 5:3 5:5 4:2 4:4 4:5" labels="interval" caption="G major, one octave: string 6 (3, 5) – string 5 (2, 3, 5) – string 4 (2, 4, 5)" />

This is a **movable** shape: slide it up to fret 5 and you have A major, to fret 8 and it is C major. Knowing the notes on string 6 (Module 0) = being able to play the major scale in every key.

## A full two-octave position

Join the two shapes and extend up to string 1 and you get a complete position between frets 2 and 5:

## Fingering for C major

**Right hand** (going up): **1 2 3 – 1 2 3 4 5**. The thumb (1) passes under the hand after finger 3 to play F.
**Left hand** (going up): **5 4 3 2 1 – 3 2 1**. Finger 3 crosses over the thumb to play A.

<Show notes="C4 D4 E4 F4 G4 A4 B4 C5" labels="interval" caption="C major — the right-hand thumb-under happens after E: thumb plays F" />

## Other keys

- **G, D, A and E major**: use **exactly the same fingering as C major**. Just remember which black keys you need.
- **F major**, right hand: **1 2 3 4 – 1 2 3 4** (finger 4 plays B♭, then the thumb passes under to C).

<Show notes="D4 E4 F#4 G4 A4 B4 C#5 D5" labels="interval" caption="D major: two black keys, F♯ and C♯, same fingering as C major" />

<Callout type="practice" title="Pick up your instrument">
Set a metronome to 60 bpm. Play the major scale up and down, one note per beat, in 3 keys: C, G and F. When it runs smoothly, move to 2 notes per beat. The goal is not speed but **not having to think** — keep your mind free for listening when you improvise.
